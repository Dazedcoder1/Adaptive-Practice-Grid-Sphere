# Motion in a Straight Line — Study Notes (Sample)

## Acceleration and equations of motion
<!-- concepts: KIN -->

**Acceleration** is the rate of change of velocity:

$$a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$$

Its SI unit is m/s². A negative acceleration along the chosen positive direction is often called a deceleration (or retardation).

For motion in a straight line with **constant acceleration**, the three equations of motion connect the initial velocity $u$, final velocity $v$, acceleration $a$, displacement $s$ and time $t$:

$$v = u + at \qquad s = ut + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2 \qquad v^2 = u^2 + 2as$$

Choose the equation that contains the quantity you want and the quantities you know. For example, a car starting from rest that accelerates at 2 m/s² for 5 s reaches $v = 0 + 2 \times 5 = 10\ \text{m/s}$ and covers $s = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 2 \times 25 = 25\ \text{m}$.

Pick a positive direction at the start and keep every velocity, acceleration and displacement consistent with it. For a ball thrown straight up, taking upwards as positive gives $a = -g = -10\ \text{m/s}^2$.

These equations only apply when the acceleration is constant. If it changes, use graphs or calculus instead.
